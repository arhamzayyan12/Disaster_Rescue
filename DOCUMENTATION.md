## 1. Abstract

This project presents an AI-Powered Disaster Management and Evacuation System designed to bridge the critical "Last-Mile Gap" in India’s disaster response infrastructure by connecting official alerts with community-driven action. Developed using a high-performance stack including **React 18**, **TypeScript**, and **Supabase**, the platform integrates real-time data from the **NDMA SACHET** platform and **NASA FIRMS**. At its core, the **Advanced Alert Classification Engine (AACE)** utilizes a weighted keyword algorithm to parse raw alert descriptions into actionable geospatial data with high confidence scores. The system features an interactive dashboard with **Leaflet-based** geospatial overlays for real-time situational awareness, alongside a specialized peer-to-peer relief module that utilizes the **Haversine formula** to match disaster victims with nearby responders. By automating the "Discovery-to-Action" pipeline and implementing strict **PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS)** for data protection, the system reduces response times from hours to seconds. This integrated approach fosters a decentralized, resilient ecosystem where official metadata transforms into proactive community-led rescue operations.

# Project Documentation: Disaster Rescue

## 2. Introduction

The global landscape of disaster management is currently undergoing a paradigm shift, driven by the increasing frequency and intensity of natural calamities attributed to climate change. In a geographically diverse country like India, which is prone to various disasters ranging from floods and cyclones to earthquakes and landslides, the efficacy of response systems is often a matter of life and death. While centralized bodies such as the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) have made significant strides in atmospheric monitoring and large-scale alert dissemination, a critical challenge remains: the "Last-Mile Gap." This gap represents the disconnect between the high-level metadata generated by government agencies and the immediate, ground-level actions required by citizens and local communities. Traditional systems often rely on a top-down approach where information flow is unidirectional, leaving localized groups without the tools for horizontal coordination. In today’s fast-paced digital era, the need for an automated, real-time, and community-centric disaster response system is more pressing than ever. This project, "Disaster Rescue," addresses these challenges by harmonizing official intelligence with grassroots peer-to-peer (P2P) coordination through an AI-powered geospatial platform.

### 2.1 Project Overview

"Disaster Rescue" is a sophisticated, full-stack web application designed to serve as a unified command center for both disaster victims and humanitarian responders. The system acts as a bridge between the National Disaster Management Authority's (NDMA) SACHET alerting system and the active community on the ground. At its technical core, the project utilizes a modern distributed architecture—incorporating React 18 for the frontend, Supabase for real-time data persistence, and Leaflet.js for geospatial visualization.

The system functions by autonomously ingesting Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) data from government feeds and processing it through the Advanced Alert Classification Engine (AACE). This engine uses weighted keyword algorithms to convert raw text descriptions into categorized, actionable markers on a live map. Beyond mere information display, the platform facilitates a unique Peer-to-Peer relief ecosystem. When a disaster occurs, individuals can post requests for specific aid (medical, food, or shelter), which are instantly broadcast to verified volunteers within a calculated proximity via the Haversine formula. By providing a real-time, interactive layer of situational awareness that includes advanced meteorological data and fire telemetry from NASA FIRMS, the project transforms static metadata into a dynamic, life-saving interface.

### 2.2 Project Purpose

The core motivation behind "Disaster Rescue" is deeply rooted in human safety and community resilience. The project was developed with a primary goal: to ensure that no individual feels isolated or helpless during a crisis. By shifting the focus from passive observation to active community participation, the project aims to solve the human challenges of disaster response through several key objectives:

*   **Saving Lives through Immediate Action:** In a disaster, every second counts. The purpose of this system is to put life-saving information directly into the hands of those who need it most, without waiting for traditional administrative chains. This ensures that a person in a flooded area or near a fire knows exactly what is happening in real-time, allowing them to make decisions that could save their life.
*   **Empowering the "Neighbors-Helping-Neighbors" Model:** Often, the fastest help comes from someone just a few streets away. This project is built to connect local heroes—regular citizens willing to help—with those in their immediate vicinity who are calling out for aid. By fostering this horizontal support network, we aim to utilize the natural human instinct to help one another in times of need.
*   **Creating Clarity in Chaos:** During a disaster, fear often stems from a lack of clear information. This project aims to replace confusion and panic with a simple, visual, and easy-to-understand map. By filtering out the "noise" and showing only what is relevant to the user’s location, the system provides a sense of calm and clear direction when it is needed most.
*   **Democratizing Safety Intelligence:** Safety should not be a luxury reserved for those with professional training. The goal of this system is to give every citizen—regardless of their technical background—the same quality of information used by experts. This allows a family to see the path of a storm or a rising river and decide for themselves when it is time to move to a safer location.
*   **Restoring Trust and Transparency in Charity:** When people offer help or donate resources, they want to be sure it reaches a human being in real need. The project provides a direct, transparent link between the responder and the victim. By removing intermediaries and providing clear status updates, the system builds a community founded on trust and direct accountability.
*   **A Unified Haven for Assistance:** Instead of searching through various news channels or social media feeds, users are provided with a single, reliable "Haven" for help. Whether someone needs a meal, medical attention, or just the location of the nearest safe shelter, the system serves as a one-stop companion for survival and recovery.

Through these human-centric goals, "Disaster Rescue" strives to build a more compassionate and resilient world where technology is used to strengthen the bonds of community and ensure the safety of every individual.

### 2.3 Project Scope

The scope of "Disaster Rescue" encompasses the development of a scalable, web-based platform tailored for disaster-prone regions, with a specific focus on the Indian subcontinent. The system is designed to be used by three primary categories of users: the general public (disaster victims), verified humanitarian volunteers, and administrative observers.

The application is highly applicable in urban high-density areas where immediate coordination of resources (like food and medical supplies) is difficult during floods, as well as in rural regions where official rescue teams may take longer to arrive. It serves as a comprehensive tool for NGOs and community groups to manage their fleet of responders and track the fulfillment of aid requests in real-time. 

However, it is important to acknowledge certain operational limitations. As a web-based application, the system’s effectiveness is contingent upon the availability of internet connectivity and power infrastructure, both of which can be compromised during extreme disasters. Furthermore, the accuracy of the platform is partially dependent on the reliability and uptime of external APIs (NDMA and NASA). Future iterations could address these by implementing progressive web app (PWA) features for offline caching and SMS-based fallback mechanisms for basic alerting.

### 2.4 Project Features

The "Disaster Rescue" system is equipped with a robust set of features designed to handle the complexities of real-time crisis management:

*   **Advanced Alert Classification Engine (AACE):** An AI-inspired logic layer that parses raw XML feeds from official sources. It applies a weighted keyword analysis (e.g., scoring "Inundation" higher than "Rain") to ensure that only significant threats are highlighted, reducing false positives and user panic.
*   **Real-Time Geospatial Dashboard:** An interactive map interface built with Leaflet.js that displays live disaster markers, NASA thermal hotspots (fires), and NDMA alerts. Users can toggle layers to see different disaster types and their specific severity levels.
*   **Meteorological Data Layer:** Provides advanced atmospheric overlays, including live wind speed, rain accumulation, and pressure shifts. This allows users to predict the path of a storm or flood and plan evacuations more effectively.
*   **Peer-to-Peer Relief Module:** A specialized interface where victims can request help for "Food," "Medical Support," or "Shelter." These requests are geofenced and matched with nearby responders, ensuring that help arrives from the nearest possible source.
*   **Verified Profile & Responder System:** A secure authentication system using Supabase Auth and PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS). This ensures that sensitive victim information is only visible to verified volunteers, preventing misuse during vulnerable times.
*   **Real-time Synchronization Engine:** Utilizing Supabase's Realtime (PostgreSQL WAL) capabilities, the system updates all active client dashboards in under 200ms whenever a new relief request is posted or a disaster alert is updated.
*   **Shelter Discovery Algorithm:** Automatically identifies nearby community centers, schools, and religious sites from OpenStreetMap data, providing refugees with immediate locations for safety.

## EXISTING & PROPOSED SYSTEM

### 3.1 EXISTING SYSTEM
The existing disaster management systems in most regions still rely heavily on conventional and manual communication channels. Authorities often use public sirens, loudspeakers, and television announcements to broadcast warnings, which may not reach every individual in time. These methods are typically decentralized and lack a unified interface for tracking real-time evacuation progress. Furthermore, the absence of interactive routing leaves citizens to rely on predetermined knowledge or local cues during a crisis. As a result, the coordination between emergency responders and the public is often delayed and inefficient.

### 3.2 EXISTING SYSTEM DISADVANTAGES
1. Lack of real-time information
2. Slow communication
3. Manual coordination
4. High chances of confusion
5. No centralized monitoring
6. Limited accessibility
7. Risk to human life
8. Poor resource management

### 3.3 PROBLEM STATEMENT
The fundamental problem lies in the inefficiency and latency of current disaster response mechanisms during critical evacuation phases. Without an automated platform, stakeholders struggle to share accurate information, leading to mass confusion and delayed mobilization. The lack of real-time tracking prevents authorities from identifying bottlenecks in evacuation routes or locating stranded individuals promptly. Consequently, there is an urgent need for a centralized, digital system that automates alerting and provides clear guidance to ensure public safety. Such a system is essential to reduce the risks to human life and improve resource allocation during catastrophes.

### 3.4 PROPOSED SYSTEM
The proposed "Disaster Evacuation Management System" is an integrated digital platform designed to provide real-time situational awareness and coordination. It functions by ingesting live data from environmental sensors and official feeds to broadcast immediate alerts to users' mobile devices. The system features an interactive geospatial interface that displays optimized evacuation routes, identified safe zones, and live disaster markers. Additionally, it offers a centralized monitoring dashboard for authorities to manage resources and track the status of evacuation zones. This shift towards an automated framework ensures faster communication and more effective disaster mitigation.

### 3.5 PROPOSED SYSTEM ADVANTAGES
• Real-time alerts
• Fast communication
• Improved safety
• Efficient evacuation
• Centralized control
• Easy monitoring
• Better resource management
